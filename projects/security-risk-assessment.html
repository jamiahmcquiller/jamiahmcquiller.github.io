Project Overview

Technology requests can introduce security, privacy, operational, and compliance considerations that must be understood before a decision is made. A security risk assessment provides a structured way to gather information about a requested technology, understand its intended use, identify potential risks, and document safeguards and outstanding questions.

USB exception requests require a related but distinct review. Removable media may be necessary for legitimate business activities, but its use can introduce risks involving sensitive information, unauthorized transfer, malware, data loss, and access outside approved systems. Reviewing these requests requires understanding the business purpose, information involved, devices or systems affected, and proposed handling arrangements.

As an IT End User Computing Technician with cybersecurity responsibilities at Genesee County, I contribute to security documentation and request review activities. This includes gathering relevant information, identifying missing details, evaluating security considerations, and documenting information needed to support an informed review.

Professional focus: Translating technology requests into structured security questions, documented risks, and reviewable information that supports consistent decision-making.

The Security Challenge

A request for a new application, technology service, or exception to an existing security restriction may arrive with incomplete information. Without a clear understanding of the intended use, data involved, affected systems, and responsible parties, reviewers may not have enough information to evaluate the request appropriately.

The challenge is to understand the business need without overlooking security obligations. This requires asking relevant questions, distinguishing confirmed facts from assumptions, identifying gaps, and documenting issues that require clarification or approval.

Key Review Objectives

  • Understand the requested technology or exception and its legitimate business purpose.
  • Identify the information being accessed, processed, stored, or transferred.
  • Consider confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy, and applicable compliance requirements.
  • Identify missing information, potential risks, and safeguards that may need to be evaluated.
  • Document review findings and outstanding questions so the appropriate stakeholders can make informed decisions.

My Role & Contributions

My contributions focus on the information-gathering, documentation, and review stages of technology risk management. I help organize request details and identify security considerations that need to be understood before a request can be evaluated or routed for approval.

1. Security Risk Assessment Documentation

I work with structured assessment documentation to capture the purpose of a technology request, its intended functionality, the responsible parties, affected systems, and relevant data considerations.

A well-organized assessment helps reviewers understand what is being requested, why it is needed, and what information is available to support the review. It also provides a place to document unanswered questions and issues requiring additional information.

2. Business Purpose and Technology Review

Understanding the business purpose is an important part of evaluating a request. I review the stated use case and consider what the technology is expected to do, which department or users need it, and which systems or workflows may be affected.

This helps distinguish the requested business outcome from the technical implementation and identifies areas where further clarification may be necessary.

3. Information Sensitivity and Privacy Considerations

I review information about the types of data a technology or exception may handle. Depending on the request, this can include personal information, health information, criminal justice information, administrative data, or other sensitive information.

Identifying data sensitivity helps establish which security and privacy questions need to be considered. It also helps reviewers understand why data handling, access restrictions, transfer methods, and retention practices may be important to the assessment.

4. Identifying Missing Information

A request may not provide enough detail to support a meaningful assessment. I identify gaps in the submitted information and help determine what clarification is needed, such as the specific business justification, data involved, affected systems, or intended access.

This helps prevent assumptions from being treated as confirmed facts and supports a more complete review.

5. USB Exception Request Reviews

I review USB exception requests to understand the requested business activity, the devices or media involved, the information being transferred, and the intended access or use. These details help establish the context needed to evaluate the request.

Where information is missing or unclear, the request may require additional clarification before it can be evaluated. The review also considers the importance of protecting sensitive information and documenting the basis for any decision.

6. Security Documentation and Stakeholder Coordination

I help organize assessment information so that relevant technical personnel, department representatives, and authorized decision-makers can understand the request and its outstanding considerations.

Clear documentation supports consistency, accountability, and traceability. It also makes it easier to understand what information was provided and which items still require review or approval.

Security Risk Assessment Workflow

The following workflow summarizes a structured approach to gathering and documenting information for a technology risk assessment. It is a high-level description of the review process, not an internal County procedure.

  1. Identify the technology.

    Establish the product, service, application, vendor, intended functionality, and business owner where that information is available.

  2. Establish the business need.

    Understand why the technology is being requested, who will use it, and which business processes it is intended to support.

  3. Identify data and affected systems.

    Gather information about the data involved, the systems or devices affected, user access, and relevant information flows.

  4. Identify risks and information gaps.

    Document potential security and privacy concerns, missing details, assumptions, and questions requiring further review.

  5. Document safeguards and considerations.

    Record relevant safeguards, proposed controls, and issues that need to be considered by the appropriate technical or business stakeholders.

  6. Support review and decision-making.

    Organize the assessment for review by the appropriate authority and document the status of outstanding questions or required approvals.

USB Exception Review Process

USB exception reviews address requests to use removable media for a defined business purpose. These requests require enough context to understand the activity being requested and the potential security implications.

Business Justification

The review begins with understanding why removable media is needed and what business activity it will support. A clear justification helps reviewers assess whether the requested activity is understood and whether additional information is required.

Devices and Information Involved

The request should identify the relevant devices or media and describe the information involved. Knowing the sensitivity of the information helps establish which data protection and access considerations may apply.

Data Movement and Access

Understanding where information originates, where it will be transferred, and who will have access helps reviewers evaluate the context of the requested transfer. Unclear data flows or access arrangements may require further clarification.

Risk Considerations and Safeguards

USB use can introduce risks involving unauthorized disclosure, loss of sensitive information, malware, and movement of data outside approved environments. Reviewers may need to consider appropriate safeguards in light of the business purpose and information involved.

Documentation and Approval

The request and its supporting information should provide a clear record of the business need, relevant security considerations, and decision status. A request should not be represented as approved unless the appropriate authority has actually approved it.

Review principle: A legitimate business need is an important part of an exception request, but it does not by itself establish that the security risks are acceptable. The request must be evaluated through the applicable review and approval process.

Tools, Frameworks & Documentation

Security Risk Assessment Templates

Structured templates help organize technology details, business purpose, data sensitivity, risk considerations, and review status. They support consistent information gathering across different types of requests.

Security Policies and Standards

Policy and standards documentation provides a basis for evaluating requests against established organizational requirements. Relevant standards should be identified based on the actual scope of the assessment rather than assumed to apply to every technology request.

CIS Controls

CIS Controls can provide a reference point for organizing security considerations and evaluating safeguards where applicable. The specific controls used should correspond to the technology, risk, and assessment scope.

Security Documentation and Records

Assessment forms, supporting documentation, exception records, and review notes help establish a clear record of the information considered during a security review. Documentation also helps communicate outstanding questions and support consistent handling of requests.

Challenges & Lessons Learned

Incomplete Requests Need Clarification

A request cannot be evaluated effectively if important information is missing. Identifying the gap and requesting clarification helps improve the quality of the review and reduces reliance on assumptions.

Business Need and Security Risk Must Both Be Considered

Technology decisions involve both operational requirements and security obligations. Understanding the business purpose is essential, but reviewers must also consider data sensitivity, potential exposure, and appropriate safeguards.

Documentation Supports Accountability

Clear records make it easier to understand the request, distinguish facts from assumptions, identify who needs to review the request, and track outstanding decisions.

Exceptions Require Deliberate Review

A security exception should be evaluated in context rather than treated as a routine administrative approval. The business purpose, information involved, risks, safeguards, and applicable approval requirements all matter.

Communication Is a Security Skill

Effective risk assessment requires translating technical concerns into clear questions and explanations that technical teams, business owners, and decision-makers can understand.
